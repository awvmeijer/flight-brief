Six changes follow from the recon but are this revision's own inferences, not recon findings. Each is marked "(inferred)" where it appears:

- Pilot reports shown on the page are fetched on request, like warnings, because no 30-minute job can be trusted.
- G-AIRMETs are fetched on request too. The recon settled on-request fetching for SIGMETs only.
- The index job stores the highest value in each neighbourhood, so the page reads one value.
- The "too far out" limit follows the longest horizon of the sources that are switched on, since Tomorrow.io's 120 h may not be there.
- The index is offered only for cruise levels FL290 to FL440, the range it was measured in.
- The wireframe is re-timed to 2 h before departure, so the warning it shows is still valid when the aircraft arrives.

## Context

Anthony has two working aviation-weather repos and wants a consumer product from them: type a flight code and a date, see the weather and turbulence on that flight. Audiences named: aviation enthusiasts, curious travellers, nervous flyers. The original pitch was "leverage Tomorrow.io's open API for turbulence hotspots."

Three facts reshaped that pitch:

1. **Tomorrow.io's free plan has no cruise-level data.** `oceanic-flight-watch/docs/RECON.md:89`: "These are surface fields only. Nothing here describes cruise-level hazards." Their flight-level turbulence (`turbAloft`) is documented as a "WAFS product" and is not on the free plan as far as anything readable shows.
2. **The category is crowded.** turbli.com, BumpySkies (free), Flying Calmly (subscription), Turby, Flight+, Turbulence Forecasts all do "flight number in, turbulence out." None publishes whether its forecasts came true.
3. **The official global turbulence grid is not open** (found in Phase 0). NOAA stopped distributing the WAFS hazard grids on public servers "on or about January 17, 2024" (Service Change Notice 23-111). Access is through WIFS, which "is not an open system" and needs approval by a State meteorological authority and the FAA.

Outcome wanted: an open-source flight page that is honest about what is known, with a public scorecard, built largely on code that already exists.

## Problem Statement

Before a flight, a curious passenger can see gate, delay and aircraft type in a flight tracker, but not what the ride will be like. The tools that do answer that sell one confident score with no sources, no disagreement, and no track record. For a forecast as shaky as single-flight turbulence, that is the wrong product. The gap is a brief that shows each source, says "nothing found" explicitly, states what can be known for each hour of the flight at this lead time, and proves over time how often it was right.

## Demand Evidence

Honest state, in Anthony's words: **"It's a hunch so far."**

- No one has asked for it. No use of the Telegram bot by strangers was claimed.
- Anthony does not look this up by hand before his own flights today.
- Indirect evidence only: six-plus existing products and at least one paid subscription show some passengers do look this up. That is demand for the category, not for this product.

- **Category:** turbli.com (up to 36 h before departure), BumpySkies (free site), several 2026 iOS apps with 1-10 scores and subscriptions. All accuracy claims found are the developers' own.
- **Implication:** the moment to attach to is "I'm checking my flight," usually the day before. A shareable web link fits that better than an app install, and the page must have real content at 24 h lead time.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- **First user:** Anthony, the avgeek ("Me, the avgeek"; "Only me so far"). A valid first user, not yet a customer.
- **Not first:** nervous flyers. No clinical evidence was found that forecasts help or harm them. A calm mode waits for real users and a careful design.
- **Narrowest wedge inside the chosen approach:** one flight page, readable in 20 seconds the day before a flight, that a curious person would paste into a group chat.

## Constraints

- **No public global turbulence grid.** The WAFS grid needs a WIFS approval that a passenger page may not get, and WIFS's terms on public display are unknown. What is open:
  - **Open GFS winds, worldwide.** From them a job computes the Ellrod TI1 index (vertical wind shear times deformation), which was itself the official WAFS product before 2020. Inputs come from NOAA's public AWS bucket, to T+384.
  - **GTG, contiguous US only.** A real turbulence forecast (eddy dissipation rate) at 3 km, keyless on NOMADS, 24 runs a day, forecast hours 0 to 18, two days kept online.
- **What the index can and cannot say.** Measured in Phase 0 at 24 to 27 h lead, at cruise level: AUC 0.76 for separating 941 moderate-or-greater pilot reports from 472 smooth ones, and 0.66 when 1,416 light reports are counted with the smooth ones. With the grid-point value the 0.76 drops to 0.70, so the page uses the highest value within 0.5 degree of the route. Flagging at 8 (units 1e-7 s^-2) catches 72% of moderate-or-greater reports and also flags 35% of smooth ones; at 12, 56% and 19%. It has no severity calibration, no mountain-wave term and no thunderstorm term. The measurement covers one autumn, the US West and the North Pacific, FL290 to FL440.
- **Wind speed is not a turbulence hint.** Wind speed alone scored AUC 0.53, which is no skill. The page never shows jet-stream speed as a reason to expect bumps.
- **Tomorrow.io (optional in Phase 3).** Free plan: 3 req/s, 25/h, 500/day, one key per account. That key is already shared: the bot budgets 20/h and the collector 8/h, which exceeds 25/h on paper. **This product has no headroom on the shared key,** and there is no self-serve way to get any: more quota and extra keys go through sales, and no price is published. The terms could not be read on a live page (bot check). From search-index fragments, a copy of the attribution clause on NASA's site and the October recon: no storing of the unaltered feed; "Powered by Tomorrow.io" shown near the data and near anything derived from it, as a link to tomorrow.io; commercial use on self-signup accounts appears prohibited. The page shows derived categories only and stores no raw values. 2 calls per leg (two airports).
- **aviationweather.gov:** no key, 100 req/min, custom User-Agent, 30 days of history. `sigmet` returns international and US domestic SIGMETs in one call (about 120 KB worldwide). `isigmet` and `airsigmet` are flagged deprecated.
- **Official warnings are short-lived.** SIGMETs are valid for hours, not days (reviewer's scan of the store: median 4 h, max 6 h; US convective SIGMETs 2 h). G-AIRMET high-level turbulence areas reach 12 h ahead and cover the contiguous US only. None of them says anything about a flight tomorrow.
- **Alaska AIRMETs carry no polygons,** so AIRMET-level hazards over Alaska are "not covered."
- **Pilot reports are an uneven yardstick.** Of 126,156 unique reports in 71 days, only 14.6% say anything about turbulence; most of the rest are automatic position reports, and silence is not a smooth-air report. 54% of cruise-level turbulence reports sit over US land. At cruise level the US West gets about 78 turbulence reports a day, Alaska, Japan and the north-east Pacific about 20 each, and every other region collected under 4. Europe, the Atlantic and the US east of 110°W were never collected. One airline files 96% of the AIREPs that mention turbulence. Pilots report rough air far more readily than smooth air, though 4,355 explicit smooth-air reports make a limited false-flag measure possible where reports are dense.
- **Flight data licences.**
  - VRS standing-data is CC0: callsign to airports, no times.
  - adsbdb's route data "may not be copied, published, or incorporated into other databases" without permission, so it is not used.
  - AeroDataBox is the only schedule source with clear terms: free tier non-commercial with attribution, about 200 lookups a month; by the recon's reading of its terms (not legal advice), content may not be kept past 7 days; a paid plan ($7.50 to $8 a month) is needed before any commercial use.
  - adsb.lol data is ODbL: attribution on any page that draws a track, and a published table of median tracks must be offered under ODbL. The code can stay MIT.
- **No rule turns a flight number into an ATC callsign.** Many airlines fly under alphanumeric callsigns that change each season (share of routes with a letter in the number: easyJet 85%, Ryanair 83%, British Airways 52%, American 7%, Singapore Airlines 1%). "Could not map" is a normal state, common on European short-haul.
- **Scheduled jobs are not punctual.** GitHub Actions is free on a public repository, but scheduled runs "can be delayed" and "may be dropped" (community measurements in 2026: median about 26 minutes late). Nothing may depend on a 30-minute cadence. Jobs are scheduled at an odd minute and can also be started by hand. Scheduled workflows are switched off after 60 days without repository activity, and keepalive actions are avoided: the best-known one was disabled by GitHub for a terms violation. GitHub's terms leave a data-collection job in a grey area; if it is ever refused, the same scripts run on any small cron host. The grid decode was measured on macOS, not on a Linux runner.
- **No LLM calls in the request path.** The brief is deterministic geometry and templates, as in `geo/encounters.py`.
- **Serverless limits:** the request function reads a store and makes a few short-timeout HTTP calls; it does not decode weather grids, pace polite HTTP clients, or keep budgets in memory (per-instance memory and `/tmp` do not persist on Vercel, per the POC README).
- **Abuse:** validate input before any upstream call, per-IP rate limit, `noindex` on `/f/` pages.
- **Wording:** "Not for flight operations" on every page. Never the words safe, unsafe, or a single overall score. Outside the GTG area, never a severity word for a forecast: the page says "elevated wind shear."
- **Solo builder, free tiers.**

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Telegram flight brief (minimal)
`/flight SQ322 14NOV` in the existing bot. Great-circle route, warnings + pilot reports + airport weather. Effort S, risk low. Was the recommendation, because it tests "does anyone look this up" fastest.

### Approach B: Flight page with a public scorecard (ideal). CHOSEN
Shareable page per flight, a forecast layer sampled along the route, source disagreement, airport weather, and a public track record. Effort L. **Estimate after review: human ~10-12 weeks / CC ~1-2 weeks of sessions, plus waiting on data access.** Not re-estimated after the recon, which removed the wait for grid access and added a second grid, a second official feed and a route table. Risk med-high.

### Approach C: Scorecard first (lateral)
No flight lookup. A weekly open report card on how the official turbulence forecast verifies against pilot reports. Effort M, risk low-med. It was the fallback if B's recon failed. The recon did not fail, so C is not needed, and its idea lives on as Phase 5.

## Recommended Approach

**B, in gated phases, on open data.** Anthony chose B over the A recommendation. The review showed the first phasing was wrong: without a forecast layer, the page is nearly empty at the lead time people actually look (the day before), because official warnings only cover the next few hours. So the forecast layer comes ahead of the page, the demand test moves to a soft launch at Phase 3, and the scorecard runs on a fixed panel of flights rather than waiting for users.

**Phase 0 settled the question B hinged on.** The official grid cannot be had without an approval, so B does not wait for it. The gap is filled by the open-GFS index worldwide and GTG over the US, and a free runner can fetch and decode both. The page has content at the hour people look, provided it says "elevated wind shear" and names a severity only where an official source does.

### Knowability, per flight hour (replaces the per-page ladder)

Each hour of the flight and each airport gets its own state, because a 13-hour flight at T-3 h straddles several horizons.

| Source | Speaks for | Otherwise shows |
|---|---|---|
| Wind-shear index (open GFS) | a flight hour at FL290 to FL440 within the fetched horizon, 54 h | "too early to know"; below FL290 "not covered" (inferred) |
| GTG turbulence forecast | a flight hour over the contiguous US within 18 h | "too early to know" over the US; not listed elsewhere |
| G-AIRMET high-level turbulence | a flight hour over the contiguous US within 12 h | "too early to know" over the US; not listed elsewhere |
| Official warnings (SIGMETs) | a flight hour the aircraft reaches while a warning is still valid | "none issued this far ahead" |
| Pilot reports | a flight hour reached within about 6 h, in a region with reports | "not covered here" |
| TAF | an airport, within about 24-30 h | "too early to know" |
| Tomorrow.io airport forecast (optional) | an airport, when its time is within 120 h | "too early to know"; the line is absent when Tomorrow.io is off |

The 54 h index horizon covers a brief issued 24 h ahead of a 14-hour flight. GFS winds run much further, but the index was measured at 24 to 27 h lead only, so the horizon is not stretched until the scorecard has numbers for longer leads. Both grid horizons are forecast hours counted from the model run, so the usable lead is shorter by the run's age: GTG files post 53 to 88 minutes after run time, and the fetch cadence adds to that.

Tomorrow.io's place: it is the already-integrated source that reaches past the TAF horizon for the two airports. The airport layer works without it (TAF/METAR), and Phase 3 launches without it if no headroom is found.

### States and page wording

| State | When | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing found | sources cover this hour and report nothing | "Nothing found in any source" |
| Too early to know | hour or airport is beyond a source's horizon | "Too early to know. Check again after {time}" |
| Not covered | no feed for this region, level or hazard | "Not covered here" |
| Source stale | cache or tile older than its TTL, or a job's heartbeat is late | "{source} last updated {age} ago" |
| Could not map | the flight number does not map to a route | "Couldn't match that flight number to a route. Enter the two airports and a departure time" |
| Departed or past | departure is in the past | "This flight has departed. Briefs are issued before departure" |
| Too far out | departure is beyond the longest horizon of the sources switched on: 5 days with Tomorrow.io, otherwise the index horizon (inferred) | "Too far out. Come back within {limit}" |
| Delays, cancellations, whether the flight runs that day | never tracked: the route table has no schedules, and the typed date and time are taken as given | "Check your airline or flight tracker" |

**How a flagged hour reads.**

- **From the index:** "Elevated wind shear," with the threshold and its measured rates printed beside it, for example "flags 72% of rough reports and 35% of smooth ones." Never light, moderate or severe.
- **From an official source:** the source's own words. GTG's category over the US, the SIGMET or G-AIRMET hazard, the pilot report's stated intensity.
- **Never from wind speed.**

**The summary line is counts of what is known, not a verdict.** Example: "1 flagged stretch (hours 2 to 3). Wind-shear index covers 14 of 14 hours."

### Brief record (identity and shape)

Identity: `(code, date, leg, issued_at)`. Date means the **scheduled local departure date at the leg's origin**; times are shown in local and UTC.

```
Brief {
  flight      {code, callsign, origin, destination,
               departure_utc, departure_local,          # stored as user input, never as API content
               cruise_fl, groundspeed_kt, assumptions[]}        # assumptions are printed on the page
  route       {kind: great_circle | track_median, n_tracks, points[]}
  hours[]     {index, from, to, state, agreement: agree | disagree | single,
               shear{model, run, layer, value, threshold, flagged} | null,
               gtg{run, edr, category} | null,          # contiguous US within 18 h
               warnings[], gairmets[], reports[]}
  near_airports[]   encounters tested surface to cruise within ~150 nm of each end
  airports    {origin{taf, tomorrow?}, destination{taf, tomorrow?}}   # side by side, derived categories
  provenance[]      {source, fetched_at, issued_at, url_without_secrets, licence}
}
```

`agreement` is computed over the sources that speak for that hour. Over the US within 18 h that means GTG against the open index, which is real disagreement between two forecasts.

### Who fetches what

| Source | Fetched by | TTL | On a miss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wind-shear index | scheduled job per GFS run, from AWS: forecast hours 6 to 54 every 3 h, about 190 MB and 90 s a run, decoded offline to compact tiles | until the next run | "source stale", then "too early to know" |
| GTG (contiguous US) | scheduled job, by byte range from NOMADS, about 67 MB a run for 5 cruise levels | until the next run fetched | line omitted with a note |
| SIGMETs (international + US) | on request, one worldwide `sigmet` call, short timeout, cached in the store | 30 min | "source stale" |
| G-AIRMETs (contiguous US) | on request when the route crosses the US, cached (inferred). One call returns one snapshot, so the full 12 h is five calls | 30 min | line omitted with a note |
| Pilot reports, for the page | on request, per route box, cached (inferred) | 30 min | "source stale" |
| Pilot reports, for scoring | daily job reading back through AWC's 30 days, so a late or dropped run loses nothing | n/a | caught up on the next run |
| TAF / METAR | on request, per airport, plain short-timeout HTTP (not `Polite`, which paces and sleeps) | 30 min | omitted with a note |
| Tomorrow.io (optional) | on request, per airport, behind a store-backed ledger | 3 h | TAF only, with a note |
| Route table | scheduled job copying VRS standing-data (CC0) into the store | 7 days | ask for airports and time |
| Departure time pre-fill (optional) | on request, AeroDataBox, never stored | not cached | the user types the time |

Store writes go to the external store (Turso or object storage), not to Git. Each job writes its run time there as a heartbeat, which is what "source stale" reads.

### Phases

- **Phase 0, Recon. Done 2026-11-12,** see `docs/RECON.md`.

  | Item | Verdict |
  |---|---|
  | (a) Official WAFS grid | Not available without approval. WIFS is an optional application |
  | (a) Open-GFS index | Workable, weaker in what it may claim |
  | (a) Public US GTG grid | Available, and was not in the first design |
  | (b) Flight resolve | Works with conditions: no open schedule times, no callsign rule |
  | (c) Tomorrow.io terms | Not readable. Needs Anthony's action |
  | (d) Scoring method | Workable with a different unit, in two regions |
  | (e) US coverage | One endpoint covers it |
  | (f) GitHub Actions cron | Capable, not punctual |

- **Phase 1, Core package and brief engine.** Extract a small core from `oceanic-flight-watch` (see Reuse).
  - **Resolver:** normalise the input (strip leading zeros, keep a suffix letter); map the airline code through VRS `airlines.csv`, where one IATA code can mean several airlines; look the callsign up in the VRS route table for the airports; otherwise "could not map," and the user types airports. The user always types or confirms the departure time.
  - **Engine:** cruise level and groundspeed default by distance band and are printed as assumptions. Per-hour knowability. Warning encounters at cruise from `sigmet`, plus a surface-to-cruise test near each airport. G-AIRMET high-level turbulence areas over the US. Reports within a corridor. Airports side by side.
  - **CLI:** `brief SQ322 2026-11-14 --dep 23:55 --as-of <time> --snapshots <dir>` returns the record above and runs offline.
- **Phase 2, Forecast layer.**
  - **Worldwide:** a scheduled job computes the Ellrod TI1 index from each GFS run in three layers (300-250, 250-200 and 200-150 hPa) and stores compact tiles. The engine takes the highest value within 0.5 degree of the route, in the layer nearest cruise level. The job can store that neighbourhood maximum directly, so the page reads one value (inferred). Tiles get smaller only if the grid is also thinned.
  - **Contiguous US:** a second job stores GTG for the cruise levels, and the engine samples it for flight hours within 18 h. Start this job early: only two days of GTG files stay online, so GTG's track record begins the day the job starts saving tiles. The index can be scored back to at least September 2026 from the AWS archive.
  - **First step:** run the decode on a Linux runner, which the recon did not test.
  - **Optional third source:** the same index from ECMWF open data, which makes "2 of 3 sources agree" possible worldwide.
- **Phase 3, Flight page and soft launch.** Server-rendered `/f/{code}/{date}/{leg}` on Vercel. Hour strip, simple map, states table, one static share image. Rate limit, `noindex`. Every brief saved with its inputs. Tomorrow.io, with its store-backed ledger and airport cache, is switched on only if headroom exists; otherwise the airports show TAF and METAR. **Soft launch to named people. The Demand criterion starts here, and Phases 4-5 proceed only if it is met or Anthony decides to continue for portfolio reasons.**
- **Phase 4, Panel and real routes.** About 20 fixed flights briefed daily at T-24 h and T-6 h, so scorecard data accumulates without users. Choose flights with numeric callsigns, most of them over the US and the North Pacific, and enter their times by hand.
  - **Tracks:** one adsb.lol callsign lookup while the flight is airborne to learn the aircraft, then one trace call within a day: about 40 metered calls a day, inside the free limit. Oceanic stretches will be gaps. A flight the job misses keeps the great-circle route.
  - **Route:** median of recent tracks (provisional: last 14 flown, same weekday not required, resampled every 20 nm), shown as "k of N recent routings cross this." Under 3 tracks: great circle, labelled. Any page that draws a track credits adsb.lol, and a published table of median tracks is offered under ODbL.
- **Phase 5, Scorecard.**
  - **Needs first:** pilot reports for the whole contiguous US. The current boxes stop at 110°W, and collection should start now, not at Phase 5 (see Waiting on Anthony).
  - **Regions:** the contiguous US and the North Pacific corridor (US West coast, Alaska, Hawaii, Japan). The other regions measured, such as the Indian Ocean and the South Pacific, print "not scored: fewer than 2 reports a day mention turbulence." Regions never collected, such as Europe and the Atlantic, print "not scored: not measured yet."
  - **Primary unit: one pilot report.** Every cruise-level report (FL290 to FL440) that states an intensity is compared with the forecasts issued 24 h and 6 h before, at the valid time within 90 minutes of the report, using the highest forecast value within 0.5 degree and the layer nearest the report.
  - **Scores, each with its sample count,** per region and per source (open index everywhere, GTG over the US): hit rate on moderate-or-greater reports at the page's flag threshold; false-flag rate on smooth reports; AUC.
  - **Secondary unit: a panel route-hour,** counted only when a report falls within 100 nm, 90 minutes and 4,000 ft. Coverage is printed next to every figure, and a route with fewer than 30 matched route-hours is not shown.
  - **Limits printed on the page:** the share of flags that were false cannot be measured, only the share of smooth reports that were flagged; intensity is a pilot's judgement; one airline dominates oceanic reports; position and time are approximate; no report means not scored, never "smooth."
- **Later, gated on evidence:** a WIFS application for the official grid, Telegram `/flight`, per-flight share cards, codeshares, a public JSON endpoint, calm mode, "tell me if my brief changes," accounts, pricing.

From `oceanic-flight-watch` (`src/oceanic_watch/`), copied into the core with credit:
- `geo/route.py`: `densify`, `timeline`, `position_at`, `unwrap`. Reused as is. The antimeridian is already handled.
- `geo/encounters.py`: `encounters`, `crossing`, `unplaced`. Reused with changes: it tests a single altitude (lines 71, 74) and silently drops hazards that expire before the aircraft arrives (lines 79-81). The passenger engine needs the climb/descent test and must report "none issued this far ahead" instead of nothing.
- `geo/hazards.py`: `from_isigmet`. Reused with a small change: a `sigmet` record has the `isigmet` shape plus three fields. US convective records have no base and a top in feet. A US non-convective domestic SIGMET was not seen in the recon, so its values are unverified.

## Reviewer Concerns (unresolved, carried forward)

- **The forecast layer is weaker than the product's rivals may hold.** The first concern, a single point of failure in the grid, is resolved by the open index. What replaces it: the index names no severity, misses mountain-wave and thunderstorm turbulence, and was measured over one autumn in two regions. NOAA's own assessment says the official grid's lead is larger against instrument measurements than against pilot reports, so services holding the official grid are ahead on that measure.
- **GTG's skill was not measured in the recon.** Only two days of files are online.
- **The yardstick and the forecast overlap unevenly by region.** Confirmed with numbers for the three boxes collected (West Pacific, East Pacific to 110°W, Indian Ocean): only the US West and the North Pacific have enough reports. A scorecard for the Indian Ocean or the South Pacific is not possible from this source. Europe and the Atlantic were never collected, so they are unknown.
- **Median track is not tomorrow's routing, and ADS-B has oceanic gaps.** Route uncertainty will always be visible on the page.
- **Many flights will not map.** European short-haul falls back to typed airports.
- **Review coverage is thin.** One review round was run on the first draft (about 10 minutes each). The first revision was never re-reviewed. The recon revision got one read against the recon, by the recon's author, and no design review.

## Verification (how to test it end to end)

- **Replay:** `brief ... --as-of --snapshots` on a past flight between two of the 29 covered airports, on a day inside the store, chosen because it crossed a known thunderstorm or turbulence warning within validity; assert entry and exit times against the core `encounters`. A quiet-day fixture asserts "nothing found."
- **Knowability:** the same flight briefed at T-5 d, T-24 h, T-6 h and T-1 h; assert each hour and airport shows the expected state, with Tomorrow.io on and off.
- **Index:** a stored tile with a known maximum 0.4 degree off the route is found, and one 0.6 degree off is not. A flight at FL250 gets "not covered."
- **GTG:** a route over the contiguous US shows a GTG line for hours within 18 h and "too early to know" beyond; a route outside the US shows none.
- **Wording:** render briefs for every fixture and assert that light, moderate and severe appear only inside text quoted from GTG, a SIGMET, a G-AIRMET or a pilot report.
- **Edge inputs:** antimeridian flight; multi-leg number; red-eye crossing midnight; leading zeros; a suffix letter; an IATA code shared by two airlines; an unmappable easyJet-style number; past date; beyond the horizon; a route over the contiguous US with a US convective SIGMET.
- **Climb and descent:** a fixture with a low-topped warning near the origin, skipped at cruise and reported under "near the airports."
- **Late jobs:** with a tile heartbeat two runs old, the page shows "source stale" and still renders.
- **Budget:** exhaust the ledger in a test; the page shows TAF only with a note.
- **Page:** render from a fixture and check with `/browse` at phone and desktop widths.
- **Scorecard:** re-run the recon's measurement through the verification job and land inside its interval (AUC 0.74 to 0.79 for the index within 0.5 degree); hand-check 10 matches against raw report text. The measurement's scripts and its one-row-per-report results are in `docs/recon/`.
- **Live:** Anthony's own next flight, briefed before departure, compared after landing.
