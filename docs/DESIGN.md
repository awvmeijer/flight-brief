## Context

Anthony has two working aviation-weather repos and wants a consumer product from them: type a flight code and a date, see the weather and turbulence on that flight. Audiences named: aviation enthusiasts, curious travellers, nervous flyers. The original pitch was "leverage Tomorrow.io's open API for turbulence hotspots."

Two facts reshaped that pitch during the session:

1. **Tomorrow.io's free plan has no cruise-level data.** `oceanic-flight-watch/docs/RECON.md:89`: "These are surface fields only. Nothing here describes cruise-level hazards." Their flight-level turbulence (WAFS Aloft) is a sales-gated premium layer that repackages public WAFS grids.
2. **The category is crowded.** turbli.com, BumpySkies (free), Flying Calmly (subscription), Turby, Flight+, Turbulence Forecasts all do "flight number in, turbulence out." None publishes whether its forecasts came true.

Outcome wanted: an open-source flight page that is honest about what is known, with a public scorecard, built largely on code that already exists.

## Problem Statement

Before a flight, a curious passenger can see gate, delay and aircraft type in a flight tracker, but not what the ride will be like. The tools that do answer that sell one confident score with no sources, no disagreement, and no track record. For a forecast as shaky as single-flight turbulence, that is the wrong product. The gap is a brief that shows each source, says "nothing found" explicitly, states what can be known for each hour of the flight at this lead time, and proves over time how often it was right.

## Demand Evidence

Honest state, in Anthony's words: **"It's a hunch so far."**

- No one has asked for it. No use of the Telegram bot by strangers was claimed.
- Anthony does not look this up by hand before his own flights today.
- Indirect evidence only: six-plus existing products and at least one paid subscription show some passengers do look this up. That is demand for the category, not for this product.

This is the weakest part of the plan. The Assignment and the Phase 3 soft launch exist to test it early, before the expensive phases.

## Status Quo

- **Anthony's own:** "A flight tracker only." There is a pre-flight habit of opening a tracker and no pre-flight weather habit. The product competes with not looking.
- **Category:** turbli.com (up to 36 h before departure), BumpySkies (free site), several 2026 iOS apps with 1-10 scores and subscriptions. All accuracy claims found are the developers' own.
- **Implication:** the moment to attach to is "I'm checking my flight," usually the day before. A shareable web link fits that better than an app install, and the page must have real content at 24 h lead time.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- **First user:** Anthony, the avgeek ("Me, the avgeek"; "Only me so far"). A valid first user, not yet a customer.
- **Not first:** nervous flyers. No clinical evidence was found that forecasts help or harm them. A calm mode waits for real users and a careful design.
- **Narrowest wedge inside the chosen approach:** one flight page, readable in 20 seconds the day before a flight, that a curious person would paste into a group chat.

## Constraints

- **Tomorrow.io free plan:** 3 req/s, 25/h, 500/day, one key per account. That key is already shared: the bot budgets 20/h and the collector 8/h, which exceeds 25/h on paper. **This product has no headroom on the shared key.** 2 calls per leg (two airports). Terms: no redistribution of the unaltered feed, "Powered by Tomorrow.io" attribution, commercial use needs a paid plan (to confirm). The page shows derived categories only, never raw feed values in a public JSON.
- **aviationweather.gov:** no key, 100 req/min, custom User-Agent, 30 days of history.
- **Official warnings are short-lived:** SIGMETs are valid for hours, not days (reviewer's scan of the store: median 4 h, max 6 h). They say nothing about a flight tomorrow.
- **`isigmet` is international only.** It carries no US domestic SIGMETs, so over the contiguous US it would give a false all-clear unless a US feed is added or the region is marked "not covered."
- **Pilot reports are an uneven yardstick:** subjective, clustered where traffic reports (reviewer's scan: 6,109 turbulence-positive reports in the eastern Pacific box, 637 western Pacific, 6 Indian Ocean). The store does hold explicit smooth-air reports (2,454 NEG vs 6,752 positive), which makes a limited false-alarm measure possible where reports are dense.
- **No LLM calls in the request path.** The brief is deterministic geometry and templates, as in `geo/encounters.py`.
- **Serverless limits:** the request function reads a store; it does not decode weather grids, pace polite HTTP clients, or keep budgets in memory (per-instance memory and `/tmp` do not persist on Vercel, per the POC README).
- **Abuse:** validate input before any upstream call, per-IP rate limit, `noindex` on `/f/` pages.
- **Wording:** "Not for flight operations" on every page. Never the words safe, unsafe, or a single overall score.
- **Solo builder, free tiers.**

## Premises (all six agreed in session)

1. The category is crowded. A seventh entrant needs a reason beyond "mine uses Tomorrow.io."
2. Tomorrow.io is the airport-weather layer, not the turbulence source. Cruise-level data comes from NOAA.
3. The wedge is honesty: show the sources, show disagreement, publish the track record.
4. Version one is for the avgeek. Calm mode comes later and carefully.
5. Ship as a web page with a shareable link per flight, plus the existing Telegram bot. No App Store yet. *(Review note: the Telegram command is deferred to "Later," see Scope cuts. Say so if you want it back in Phase 3.)*
6. Open source first. "Business" stays open until five people who aren't Anthony use it twice.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Telegram flight brief (minimal)
`/flight SQ322 14NOV` in the existing bot. Great-circle route, warnings + pilot reports + airport weather. Effort S, risk low. Was the recommendation, because it tests "does anyone look this up" fastest.

### Approach B: Flight page with a public scorecard (ideal). CHOSEN
Shareable page per flight, turbulence grid sampled along the route, source disagreement, airport weather, and a public track record. Effort L. **Revised estimate after review: human ~10-12 weeks / CC ~1-2 weeks of sessions, plus waiting on data access.** Risk med-high.

### Approach C: Scorecard first (lateral)
No flight lookup. A weekly open report card on how the official turbulence forecast verifies against pilot reports. Effort M, risk low-med. The fallback if B's recon fails.

## Recommended Approach

**B, in gated phases.** Anthony chose B over the A recommendation. The review showed the first phasing was wrong: without the turbulence grid, the page is nearly empty at the lead time people actually look (the day before), because official warnings only cover the next few hours. So the grid moves ahead of the page, the demand test moves to a soft launch at Phase 3, and the scorecard runs on a fixed panel of flights rather than waiting for users.

**B's viability hinges on Phase 0(a).** If the grid cannot be obtained and the open-data fallback is too weak to be useful, stop and do C instead.

### Knowability, per flight hour (replaces the per-page ladder)

Each hour of the flight and each airport gets its own state, because a 13-hour flight at T-3 h straddles several horizons.

| Source | Speaks for | Otherwise shows |
|---|---|---|
| Tomorrow.io airport forecast | an airport, when its time is within 120 h | "too early to know" |
| Turbulence grid | a flight hour within the model's horizon (about 36 h, to confirm) | "too early to know" |
| TAF | an airport, within about 24-30 h | "too early to know" |
| Official warnings | a flight hour the aircraft reaches while a warning is still valid | "none issued this far ahead" |
| Pilot reports | a flight hour reached within about 6 h, in a region with reports | "not covered here" |

Tomorrow.io's place: it is the already-integrated source that reaches past the TAF horizon for the two airports. The airport layer still works without it (TAF/METAR), which matters given the key budget.

### States and page wording

| State | When | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing found | sources cover this hour and report nothing | "Nothing found in any source" |
| Too early to know | hour or airport is beyond a source's horizon | "Too early to know. Check again after {time}" |
| Not covered | no feed for this region or hazard | "Not covered here" |
| Source stale | cache older than its TTL | "{source} last updated {age} ago" |
| Flight not found | code cannot be mapped, or not operating | "Couldn't find that flight. Enter the two airports and a departure time" |
| Departed or past | departure is in the past | "This flight has departed. Briefs are issued before departure" |
| Too far out | more than 5 days ahead | "Too far out. Come back within 5 days" |
| Delays, cancellations | never tracked | "Check your airline or flight tracker" |

**The summary line is counts of what is known, not a verdict.** Example: "1 flagged stretch (hours 3 to 4). 2 of 3 sources agree. Forecast covers 14 of 14 hours."

### Brief record (identity and shape)

Identity: `(code, date, leg, issued_at)`. Date means the **scheduled local departure date at the leg's origin**; times are shown in local and UTC.

```
Brief {
  flight      {code, callsign, origin, destination, departure_utc, departure_local,
               cruise_fl, groundspeed_kt, assumptions[]}        # assumptions are printed on the page
  route       {kind: great_circle | track_median, n_tracks, points[]}
  hours[]     {index, from, to, state, agreement: agree | disagree | single,
               grid{}, warnings[], reports[]}
  near_airports[]   encounters tested surface to cruise within ~150 nm of each end
  airports    {origin{taf, tomorrow}, destination{taf, tomorrow}}   # side by side, derived categories
  provenance[]      {source, fetched_at, issued_at, url_without_secrets}
}
```

### Who fetches what

| Source | Fetched by | TTL | On a miss |
|---|---|---|---|
| SIGMETs (international + US) | scheduled job, one worldwide call each | 30 min | "source stale" |
| Pilot reports | scheduled job, per region box | 30 min | "source stale" |
| Turbulence grid | scheduled job per model run, decoded offline to compact tiles | until next run | "too early to know" |
| TAF / METAR | on request, per airport, plain short-timeout HTTP (not `Polite`, which paces and sleeps) | 30 min | omitted with a note |
| Tomorrow.io | on request, per airport, behind a store-backed ledger | 3 h | TAF only, with a note |
| Flight resolve | on request | 7 days | ask for airports and time |

### Phases

- **Phase 0, Recon (no product code).** `docs/RECON.md` in the new repo, same standard as the existing one. Settle:
  - (a) Turbulence grid: access, terms, format, horizon, and whether a scheduled job can decode it. Fallback if refused: wind-shear context from open GFS, never called a turbulence forecast. **Gate for the whole of B.**
  - (b) Flight resolve: a source for scheduled departure times (adsbdb gives airports but no times, per the reviewer's spot check); rules for IATA flight number to ICAO callsign, leading zeros, suffixes, alphanumeric callsigns; whether track history can be had per callsign or only as bulk dumps. **Gate for Phase 1's input form.**
  - (c) Tomorrow.io: terms for a public page showing derived categories; how to get headroom (trim the bot's budget, a paid plan, or ask them). **Gate for Phase 3.**
  - (d) Scoring: region, unit, matching window, how to report where reports are thin.
  - (e) US coverage: the US domestic SIGMET and G-AIRMET feeds, or mark the region "not covered."
  - (f) GitHub Actions cron as the job host: schedule reliability and store writes.
- **Phase 1, Core package and brief engine.** Extract a small core from `oceanic-flight-watch` (see Reuse). Resolver with normalisation and a "could not map" state. Input is code + date + local departure time, pre-filled when a source knows it. Cruise level and groundspeed default by distance band and are printed as assumptions. Per-hour knowability. Warning encounters at cruise, plus a surface-to-cruise test near each airport. Reports within a corridor. Airports side by side. CLI: `brief SQ322 2026-11-14 --dep 23:55 --as-of <time> --snapshots <dir>` returns the record above and runs offline.
- **Phase 2, Turbulence grid.** Scheduled job decodes each model run into compact tiles; the engine samples a tile per flight hour at cruise level. Or the 0(a) fallback.
- **Phase 3, Flight page and soft launch.** Server-rendered `/f/{code}/{date}/{leg}` on Vercel reading the store only. Hour strip, simple map, states table, one static share image. Store-backed Tomorrow.io ledger and airport cache. Rate limit, `noindex`. Every brief saved with its inputs. **Soft launch to named people. The Demand criterion starts here, and Phases 4-5 proceed only if it is met or Anthony decides to continue for portfolio reasons.**
- **Phase 4, Panel and real routes.** About 20 fixed flights briefed daily at T-24 h and T-6 h, so scorecard data accumulates without users. For panel flights, route = median of recent tracks (provisional: last 14 flown, same weekday not required, resampled every 20 nm), shown as "k of N recent routings cross this." Under 3 tracks: great circle, labelled.
- **Phase 5, Scorecard.** Unit: one route-hour of a panel brief at a fixed lead time. Scores the grid forecast along the planned route, not the warnings. Provisional match window, to be set in 0(d): reports within 100 nm, 1 h and 4,000 ft. Public page with hit rate, the limited false-alarm measure from smooth-air reports, per-region coverage, and what is not scored.
- **Later, gated on evidence:** Telegram `/flight`, per-flight share cards, codeshares, a public JSON endpoint, calm mode, "tell me if my brief changes," accounts, pricing.

### Reuse (checked against the code by me and by the reviewer)

Every named function exists with the stated signature. The fit is partial, so **Phase 1 starts by extracting a small core package into the new repo**, not by pinning `oceanic-flight-watch`: that repo has no git remote, pulls in `mcp` and `anthropic`, and several reused modules need edits.

From `oceanic-flight-watch` (`src/oceanic_watch/`), copied into the core with credit:
- `geo/route.py`: `densify`, `timeline`, `position_at`, `unwrap`. Reused as is. The antimeridian is already handled.
- `geo/encounters.py`: `encounters`, `crossing`, `unplaced`. Reused with changes: it tests a single altitude (lines 71, 74) and silently drops hazards that expire before the aircraft arrives (lines 79-81). The passenger engine needs the climb/descent test and must report "none issued this far ahead" instead of nothing.
- `geo/hazards.py`: `from_isigmet`. Reused as is; a US-feed parser is new.
- `models.py`: `Hazard`, `Encounter`, `Waypoint` as is. **`Flight` does not fit as is**: `aircraft`, `cruise_fl`, `groundspeed_kt`, `etops_minutes` and `alternates` are all required. The core gets a slimmer passenger flight with defaults and printed assumptions.
- `sources/awc.py`: the endpoints and record handling. `Polite`'s pacing and retry sleeps stay in scheduled jobs only.
- `snapshots.py`: the `Envelope` model. Storage is filesystem-only, so hosted briefs go in a new store table with inputs embedded.
- `wx.py`: the source-stale pattern.
- The snapshot store: for building the scoring method. Coverage is narrower than the first draft implied: TAF/METAR for 29 Pacific and Indian Ocean airports, reports for three ocean boxes, data ending 2026-10-15. Replay fixtures are limited to that coverage.

From `tomorrow-alert-poc` (`src/alert_poc/`):
- `web.py` `create_web_app`, `app.py`, `vercel.json`: the deployment pattern.
- `store.py` `TursoBackend` / `SqliteBackend`: the backend, with new tables (briefs, caches, ledger). The existing schema is bot tables.
- `weather.py` `WeatherService.forecast`: the fetch. `Budget` is per-instance memory and must become store-backed. `resolve_place` is not used (it costs a call).

New code: resolver, passenger flight model, per-hour knowability, climb/descent test, US-feed parser, report corridor matcher, grid job and sampler, page and map, ledger, panel job, verification job, scorecard page.

## Reviewer Concerns (unresolved, carried forward)

- B has a single point of failure in the grid (Open Question 1). No amount of design fixes that; only recon does.
- The yardstick and the forecast overlap unevenly by region. A scorecard for the Indian Ocean may never be possible from this source.
- Median track is not tomorrow's routing, and ADS-B has oceanic gaps. Route uncertainty will always be visible on the page.
- Only one review round was run (about 10 minutes each). The revised doc has not been re-reviewed.

## Success Criteria

- **Personal:** Anthony uses it for his next 3 flights and afterwards writes down how the brief compared with the ride.
- **Demand:** within 30 days of the Phase 3 soft launch, 5 named people other than Anthony ask for a brief on two separate occasions. Counted from Anthony's own list of people, not analytics (there are no accounts).
- **Wedge:** scorecard public with at least 500 scored route-hours across at least 10 panel routes, method and limits stated.
- **Craft:** every brief reproducible from its saved inputs; every state in the table has a test; warm-cache page in under 5 s.
- **Business go/no-go at 60 days after soft launch:** 25 returning people, or one unsolicited "can I pay / can I get alerts." Below that, it stays an open-source portfolio piece, which is a fine outcome.

## Verification (how to test it end to end)

- **Replay:** `brief ... --as-of --snapshots` on a past flight between two of the 29 covered airports, on a day inside the store, chosen because it crossed a known thunderstorm or turbulence warning within validity; assert entry and exit times against the core `encounters`. A quiet-day fixture asserts "nothing found."
- **Knowability:** the same flight briefed at T-5 d, T-24 h, T-6 h and T-1 h; assert each hour and airport shows the expected state.
- **Edge inputs:** antimeridian flight; multi-leg number; red-eye crossing midnight; alphanumeric callsign; unmappable code; past date; more than 5 days out; a route over the contiguous US (US feed or "not covered").
- **Climb and descent:** a fixture with a low-topped warning near the origin, skipped at cruise and reported under "near the airports."
- **Budget:** exhaust the ledger in a test; the page shows TAF only with a note.
- **Page:** render from a fixture and check with `/browse` at phone and desktop widths.
- **Scorecard:** run the verification job over the backfilled month and hand-check 10 matches against raw report text.
- **Live:** Anthony's own next flight, briefed before departure, compared after landing.

## The Assignment

**Before any product code: this week, pick 3 real upcoming flights (yours, or people you know flying in the next two weeks). For each, look it up on turbli.com and on BumpySkies the day before, screenshot what they say, and after landing ask the passenger how the ride actually went.**

Write down two things per flight: where the forecast was wrong or unclear, and what you wanted to know that it didn't show.

**Decision rule:**
- If you can't find three people who care enough to look, that is the demand answer. Build C as a portfolio piece or stop.
- If the incumbents matched the ride and nothing felt missing on all three, the honesty wedge is weak. Rethink before Phase 1.
- If at least one forecast was wrong or left you wanting the sources, proceed, and those three people are your Phase 3 soft launch.
